Game design document · v0.1 concept

  • Catch new farm animals and bring them to your music-minded farm. They will be your band.
  • Every animal has its own voice, stats and musical talents. Listen to them and find the best line-up.
  • Put them in pens, press Play, and conduct the best Moosic in the world.

Core loop

  1. CatchAnimals wander the meadow. Rarer ones are harder to lasso but sing better.

  2. HouseEach animal goes into a pen with a style, a capacity and acoustics.

  3. ConductPress Concert. Each pen sings a solo, left to right in storage order, then all pens join in the tutti as one orchestra.

  4. ScoreSolo quality, affinity and pen fit, plus how well the orchestra works as a whole, become a grade. Rearrange and go back to step 1.

Technical notes

Music
Solos: progression C – Am – F – G – C, one chord per pen, two 4/4 bars per pen. Melody uses the C major pentatonic, so the band always stays in key.
Tutti
Five bars with every pen at once: C – Am – F – G, then a held C chord. Each pen keeps its style, so Drone pens hold the floor, Rhythm pens keep time, Melody pens carry the tune and Chorus pens fill the chords. Volume per pen is scaled by 1/√pens.
Voices
Cow: two detuned saws, opening low-pass "moo" and vibrato. Pig: square with pitch drop plus noise snort. Sheep: saw with 8.5 Hz tremolo "baa". Duck: saw through a narrow band-pass, very short.
Stats → sound
Low pitch detunes each note up to ±50 cents. Low timing shifts notes up to ±70 ms. Power sets volume. Acoustics add reverb.
Ranges
Cow C2–E3, pig A2–C4, sheep G3–A4, duck E4–G5.

Open questions

  1. Progression: do concert scores unlock more pens, better acoustics and new species?
  2. Catching skill: should the lasso be a rhythm minigame that teaches timing before the concert?
  3. Song structure: fixed progression per concert, or songs picked from a songbook?
  4. Voices: real animal recordings pitched to notes, or stylized synths like this prototype?
  5. Pen order: can the player also reorder pens, or only the animals inside them?
  6. Audience: do crowds have tastes that change the scoring weights per concert?
